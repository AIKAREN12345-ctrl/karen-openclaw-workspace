## 1. DEFINITION & CHARACTERISTICS

### What is Organizational Culture?
- **Definition**: A common perception held by the organization's members; a system of shared meaning
- It's about:
  - The personality of the organization
  - How we do things
  - What's accepted and what's not
  - Norms, traditions, rituals
  - **Casual vs Formal** environments

### Why is it Important?
- It gives the company a **baseline to work under**

### Key Properties:
- Culture is **learned and created** over a period of time (sometimes organically)
- It can be **difficult to change**

### Value Dimensions of Culture (Robbins)
1. Innovation and risk taking
2. Attention to detail
3. Outcome orientation
4. People orientation
5. Team orientation
6. Aggressiveness
7. Stability

### Culture vs. Formalization
- A strong culture increases behavioral consistency and can act as a **substitute for formalization**

### OC vs. National Culture
- **National culture has greater impact** on employees than their organization's culture
- Nationals selected to work for foreign companies may be atypical of the local/native population

## 2. COMPONENTS / ELEMENTS OF CULTURE

### 1. VALUES ⭐
- Beliefs in what's good for the organization
- **Influence behaviour**
- **Stated values vs Implicit values**
- Idealistic vs influential
- Example: Value of customer service

### 2. NORMS ⭐
- **Unwritten rules of behaviour**
- Types:
  - Performance norms
  - Appearance norms
  - Social norms
  - Allocation of resources norms

### 3. ARTEFACTS
- Outward signs that show work culture
- Physical manifestations of culture

### 4. ROUTINES
- Rituals (social aspects + training)

### 5. SYMBOLS/STORIES
- Stories = method of passing culture

### 6. CEREMONIES
- Award ceremonies, formal presentations

---

## 3. TYPES OF CULTURE

### Dominant Culture
- Expresses the core values shared by a majority of the organization's members

### Subcultures
- Minicultures within an organization
- Typically defined by department designations and geographical separation

### Core Values
- The primary or dominant values accepted throughout the organization

### Strong Culture
- A culture in which the core values are intensely held and widely shared
- Cultures convey **sense of identity for members** ⭐

---

## 4. CLASSIFYING ORGANIZATIONAL CULTURE

### A. HANDY'S 4 GENERIC CULTURAL MODELS ⭐

1. **Power Culture**
   - Semi-autocratic
   - "What does the boss want?"
   - Change is directed from top

2. **Role Culture**
   - People can get set in their ways

3. **Task Culture**
   - Flexibility, whatever it takes to do the job

4. **Person-Centered Culture**
   - "What is my agenda?"

### B. DEAL & KENNEDY'S 4 CULTURES ⭐
Determined by 2 factors:
- **Risk** associated with organization's activities
- **Feedback** and how quickly employees receive it on their performance

| Culture Type | Risk Level | Feedback Speed | Characteristics |
|-------------|------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Tough Guy/Macho** | High | Rapid | Stress on failure, rapid turnover |
| **Work Hard/Play Hard** | Low | High | Balanced culture |
| **Bet Your Company** | High | Low | Survival depends on it |
| **Process** | Low | Slow | Step by step |

## 5. FUNCTIONS OF ORGANIZATIONAL CULTURE

### Positive Functions:
1. **Defines boundaries** between one organization and others
2. **Conveys a sense of identity** for its members
3. **Facilitates commitment** to something larger than self-interest
4. **Enhances stability** of the social system
5. **Serves as sense-making and control mechanism** for fitting employees into the organization

### Culture as a Liability (Dysfunctional Effects):
1. **Barrier to change**
2. **Barrier to diversity**
3. **Barrier to acquisitions and mergers**

---

## 6. HOW CULTURE BEGINS

### Role of Founders:
1. Founders hire and keep only **like-minded employees**
2. Founders **indoctrinate and socialize** these employees to their mindset
3. Founders' behavior acts as **role models**, encouraging employees to identify with them and internalize their beliefs & values

### How Culture Develops:
- By the founders, how they behave
- Act as role models
- Important events, trauma, bonding
- Critical events
- Develops from social norms and natural boundary building
- External environment

## 7. KEEPING CULTURE ALIVE

### Three Key Mechanisms:

#### 1. Selection
- Concern with how well individuals **fit**
- Provides information to candidates about the organization

#### 2. Top Management
- Senior executives help establish **behavioral norms** that are adopted by the company
- **Key role in setting the vision** ⭐

#### 3. Socialization
- The process that helps **new employees adapt** to the organization's culture

---

## 8. STAGES OF SOCIALIZATION

### 1. Pre-arrival Stage
- Process that occurs **before** a new employee joins the organization
- Period of learning in the socialization process

### 2. Encounter Stage
- New employee sees what the organization is **really like**
- Confronts when expectations & reality might differ

### 3. Metamorphosis Stage
- New employee **changes and adjusts** to the work, work group, and organization

## 10. CONTRASTING ORGANIZATIONAL CULTURES (Case Examples)

### Organization A (Traditional/Conservative)
- Manufacturing firm with extensive documentation requirements
- "Good managers" provide detailed data to support recommendations
- Creative decisions incurring significant change or risk are NOT encouraged
- Managers of failed projects are openly criticized and penalized
- Philosophy: "If it ain't broke, don't fix it"
- Extensive rules and regulations
- Close supervision to ensure no deviations
- High productivity focus regardless of employee morale/turnover impact
- Work designed around individuals with distinct departments
- Performance evaluations emphasize individual effort
- Seniority is primary factor for pay raises and promotions

### Organization B (Innovative/Risk-Taking)
- Manufacturing firm encouraging and rewarding risk taking and change
- Decisions based on intuition valued as much as rationalized ones
- History of experimenting with new technologies
- Regularly introduces innovative products
- Employees with good ideas encouraged to "run with it"
- Failures treated as "learning experiences"
- Market-driven and rapidly responsive to customer needs
- Few rules and regulations
- Loose supervision (trust-based)
- High productivity through treating people right
- Reputation as a good place to work
- Job activities designed around work teams
- Team members encouraged to interact across functions and authority levels
- Bonuses based on achievement of goals
- Considerable autonomy in choosing means to attain goals

## 12. REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES

### Samsung's Culture Problem (Case Study)
- **Issue**: "Dominant engineering culture" suggests production rather than marketing orientation
- Senior executives distrust innovation, preferring to focus on "selling boxes"
- Confusion and overlap between competing divisions
- Short-term interests of promoting hardware trump long-term platform building
- Result: Little differentiation from other Android phone makers
