# AI Security & Privacy Best Practices — 2026

## Findings

1. **Agent access control gap**: 82% of enterprises have deployed AI agents but only 44% have security policies for them; the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 identifies tool misuse, goal hijacking, and excessive agency as the highest-impact risks, and experts recommend sandboxing, least-privilege tool governance, and human-in-the-loop approval for sensitive actions.

2. **Prompt injection is the dominant real-world attack**: Adversaries exploited GenAI tools at 90+ organizations in 2025 via prompt injection (CVE-2025-32711), and because traditional firewalls and signature scanners cannot distinguish malicious prompts from legitimate queries, defense relies on layered context isolation, output validation, and input/output filtering at an AI gateway.

3. **AI supply chain and data privacy are board-level concerns**: Model poisoning, backdoored pre-trained weights, and RAG data source manipulation are documented supply-chain threats; organizations should implement PII scanning on inputs and outputs, minimize data sent to models, and maintain an AI Bill of Materials (AIBOM) to verify model provenance and checksums.
