### Finding 1: OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026

Developed through collaboration with 100+ industry experts, it provides actionable guidance for securing AI agents that plan, act, and make decisions across complex workflows. This is the foundational security reference for agentic AI deployments in 2026.

**Source:** OWASP Gen AI Security Project - https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/

### Finding 2: AI Agents Must Be Treated as First-Class Identities
Microsoft's 2026 security priorities emphasize that every AI agent should be managed with the same rigor as human identities. This includes inventorying agents, assigning clear ownership, governing access permissions, and applying consistent security standards. Organizations face "agent sprawl" risks similar to shadow IT, where unsanctioned AI systems can access data and act autonomously. Zero Trust principles apply equally to AI agents and human employees.

**Key controls:**
- Agent-level identity and RBAC with scoped, just-in-time permissions
- Human sponsors required to govern agent identity and lifecycle
- Conditional Access policies to block risky agents
- Monitoring agent interactions with external services

**Source:** Microsoft Security Blog (January 2026) - https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/01/20/four-priorities-for-ai-powered-identity-and-network-access-security-in-2026/

### Finding 3: Human-in-the-Loop Remains Essential
IBM's agentic AI security guide emphasizes that human oversight is critical for AI agents, especially given legal requirements like the EU AI Act's Article 14 which mandates human oversight for high-risk AI applications. Organizations must design policies determining when agents operate autonomously versus when they escalate to humans.

**Implementation approaches:**
- Conservative: Agents halt until human approval
- Flexible: Proceed asynchronously while awaiting human input
- Selective: Full autonomy in low-risk scenarios, human escalation for high-risk circumstances

**Source:** IBM Think (February 2026) - https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/agentic-ai-security

### Finding 4: Data Poisoning is a Critical Threat
Research shows that as few as five poisoned texts inserted into a database of millions can manipulate AI responses with a 90% success rate. Security professionals must think not just about AI outputs, but inputs as well—all training data should be treated as effectively sensitive data. This applies throughout the full machine learning lifecycle.

**Source:** IBM Think / Arxiv "Poisoned RAG" research (February 2024) - https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/agentic-ai-security

---

### Finding 5: Secure the "Action Layer" - What Agents DO
Unlike traditional AI where risks center on model quality (accuracy, drift, bias), agentic AI threats come from what agents *do*: the APIs they call, functions they invoke. Special attention must be paid to:
- **Orchestration agents:** Vulnerable to prompt injection and unauthorized access
- **Sub-agents:** Risk privilege escalation and over-permissioning
- **Physical-world interactions:** Threats extend beyond digital harms into real-world consequences

Strict validation protocols and monitoring solutions are essential for high-impact use cases.

**Source:** IBM Think (February 2026) - https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/agentic-ai-security

### Summary: Core Security Principles for AI Agents in 2026

1. **Identity Management:** Treat agents as first-class identities with RBAC and lifecycle governance
2. **Least Privilege:** Implement sandboxing, compartmentalization, and just-in-time access
3. **Human Oversight:** Maintain human-in-the-loop for high-risk scenarios per regulatory requirements
4. **Data Integrity:** Protect against data poisoning by treating all training data as sensitive
5. **Action Layer Security:** Monitor and validate what agents DO, not just what they say
6. **Zero Trust Everywhere:** Extend Zero Trust principles to AI agents with continuous validation
