## Key Finding 1: Multimodal AI Agents Are Moving from Labs to Production
Multimodal AI agents that process text, images, audio, and video simultaneously are now production-ready in 2026. Key drivers: vision-model accuracy matching human performance on many tasks, real-time latency becoming viable, and API costs dropping 60–80% since 2023. Industries transforming include healthcare (radiology assistants), customer service (visual product support), and field service (real-time repair guidance).

*Source: AI Agents Plus, March 2026*

---

## Key Finding 2: Native Speech-to-Speech Models Are Challenging Cascaded Pipelines
The dominant voice-agent architecture (STT → LLM → TTS) is being disrupted by native speech-to-speech models like OpenAI’s Realtime API and Gemini 2.5 native audio. Trade-offs:
- **Cascaded:** ~465–800 ms latency, high debuggability, flexible component swapping
- **Speech-to-speech:** ~250–300 ms latency, lower observability, vendor lock-in

For most production systems in 2026, cascaded pipelines remain the pragmatic choice, but speech-to-speech is compelling where sub-300 ms response time is critical.

*Source: Chanl Blog, March 2026*

---

## Key Finding 3: Video Generation Crossed the Commercial Threshold in 2026
Video was the last modality to reach production quality; 2026 is the year it became commercially viable. Capabilities now include:
- Script-to-1080p video generation comparable to mid-tier motion graphics
- Automated editing reducing 4–6 hour social clips to 30–45 minutes of review
- Localization into 15+ languages with lip-sync dubbing in hours
- Cost drop from $5,000–$15,000 per 60-second agency video to $50–$200 in compute

*Source: Agentmelt, March 2026*
